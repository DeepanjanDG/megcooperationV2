MECOFED

The Meghalaya State Cooperative Marketing and Consumers’ Federation Limited — marketing the produce of affiliated societies and supplying inputs and consumer goods.

The Meghalaya State Cooperative Marketing and Consumers’ Federation Limited — MECOFED, or the Federation — is a cooperative organisation registered under the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act in 1975–76, after taking over the assets and liabilities of the erstwhile Assam Hills Development Corporation.

MECOFED is an autonomous cooperative organisation managed by a Board of Directors elected by its members at the Annual General Body Meeting, as provided in its bye-laws.

Under the bye-laws, management rests with a Board of 15 members: the elected Chairman, the elected Vice Chairman, the Managing Director, five ex-officio members nominated by the State Government, and two members representing the Central Government. The authorised share capital of MECOFED is Rs. 25,00,00,000 (twenty-five crore).

Business activities

  1. Procurement and sale of surplus agricultural produce and minor forest produce — wild pipple, turmeric, ginger, jute, potato seeds and similar items.
  2. Sale of LPG, SK oil and cement.
  3. Supply of office stationery, furniture and fixtures and computer peripherals to State and Central Government offices, together with supply of tyres, tubes and motor vehicle batteries to State Government departments.

MECOFED has outlets at Jowai, Sohra, Nongstoin, Tura and Phulbari.

Strengths, weaknesses and strategy

Strengths

Owns physical infrastructure in most district headquarters — Shillong, Tura, Jowai, Nongstoin, Williamnagar and Phulbari. Has downsized its workforce through a golden handshake scheme, and the Board of Directors has banned fresh recruitment.

Weaknesses

Overstaffing, loss of business to competitors, an existence that is not purely profit-driven, a heavy debt burden, and heavy expenditure on overheads.

Strategy

  • Entering new areas of business such as dairy products, pharmaceuticals and consumer goods.
  • Establishing forward linkages with national cooperatives and producers of tradable items.
  • Establishing proper backward linkages with grassroots consumer and marketing cooperative societies, with government as facilitator.
  • Winning back business that has been lost.
  • Enhancing business in minor forest produce and surplus agricultural produce, including cashew nut, honey and wax, turmeric and sohiong.
  • Right-sizing the workforce.
  • Skill upgrading and training for remaining employees through short-term orientation courses in marketing, salesmanship and management.
  • Computerisation of business and administration.
  • A business turnover target of Rs. 15 crore within two years, and Rs. 20 crore thereafter.

Turnover, 2005–06 and 2006–07

Annual business turnover for 2005–2006 was Rs. 914.22 lakh, and the target for 2006–2007 was provisionally fixed at Rs. 1,000.25 lakh.

Business at that time consisted of the sale of fertilisers and bone meal; procurement of minor forest produce and surplus agricultural produce of the state, chiefly wild pipple, mustard seeds and jute; and the sale of SK oil, LPG and cement, together with government supply of office stationery, computer peripherals, tyres, tubes and batteries.

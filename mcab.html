Meghalaya Cooperative Apex Bank

The state’s apex cooperative bank, providing credit to societies and banking services to the public across Meghalaya.

The Meghalaya Cooperative Apex Bank provides credit facilities to cooperative societies registered under the Cooperative Societies Act in force in Meghalaya, and extends banking services to the public through its branch network.

The Bank has been managed by an elected Board since its inception, and its Managing Director is a professional banker. It has implemented ICDP in East Khasi Hills, Jaintia Hills and West Garo Hills under NCDC assistance, and enjoys a strong reputation among cooperatives.

Loan schemes

Crop loans

Short-term loans for seasonal agricultural operations, in cash and kind, provided to farmers through service cooperative societies across Meghalaya as per approved scales of finance, under NCL, cash credit systems and Kisan Credit Cards.

Term loans

Medium and long-term loans extended to farmers through affiliated service cooperative societies for allied agricultural activity: land development, minor irrigation, farm machinery, poultry, goat rearing, pisciculture, dairy, horticulture and plantation.

Cash credit accommodation

Provided to cooperative societies for procurement and marketing of agricultural and minor forest produce, and for dealing in consumer goods.

Housing loans

Extended to salaried persons for construction of residential houses in CD Block headquarters and other selected areas, against adequate security.

Transport vehicle loans

MT loans to transport societies and to educated unemployed youth, for self-employment generation and to extend easy mobility to the people of the state.

Consumer durables loans

For salaried persons, to purchase televisions, radios, refrigerators, two-wheelers, musical instruments, cooking gas, furniture and other approved items.

Cash credit facilities

Government-appointed wholesalers are extended cash credit and loan facilities for dealing in controlled commodities, against adequate security.

Tourism development term loans

To encourage young and enterprising entrepreneurs and unemployed persons to create self-employment opportunities through tourism development.

Deposit-linked housing loans

For regular constituents of the Bank, to construct residential houses with financial assistance from the Bank.

Integrated Village Development Scheme

Helps form homogeneous groups of 5 to 20 members in rural areas in the cooperative sector, and extends loan assistance for socially useful and economically viable activity.

Personal loans

Salaried persons are provided personal loans for any bona fide, non-speculative need, as overdraft facilities against adequate security.

Educational loans

Provided to parents and deserving students for higher studies in India or abroad, against adequate security.

Loans for professionals and technocrats

Credit facilities for doctors, lawyers, technocrats and other professionals to set up clinics, consultancy firms and similar practices, against adequate security.

Other schemes and services

  • Financial assistance to urban banks, weavers’ cooperatives, industrial cooperatives and joint farming societies.
  • Conversion of short-term agricultural loans affected by natural calamities into medium-term loans.
  • Implementation of the comprehensive crop insurance scheme for the benefit of farmers.
  • Consumption loans to farmers and weaker sections for approved purposes.
  • Godown loans to service cooperative societies.

The Bank has arrangements for issue of bank drafts and cheques throughout Assam and in Kolkata, Mumbai, Delhi and Chennai.

Highlights and achievements

  1. The Bank was established in 1971 with the formation of Meghalaya after the bifurcation of Assam. It commenced operations with two branches, at Shillong and Tura, and has since grown to a wide network across the state. It has mobilised deposit resources of more than Rs. 378.19 crore and advanced over Rs. 118.22 crore for development activity.
  2. As the pioneering cooperative banking institution in Meghalaya, it has taken banking to people’s doorsteps and nurtured banking habits, shifting saving from traditional methods to modern banking facilities.
  3. Through judicious deployment of resources it has maintained the highest credit-deposit ratio in the state, about 32 per cent, against about 14 per cent for commercial and public sector banks.
  4. It provided debt relief of Rs. 9.81 crore to more than 54,000 farmers under the Agricultural and Rural Debt Relief Scheme, 1990, sponsored by the Government of India, making them debt-free and eligible to borrow again.
  5. It introduced all-women bank branches, staffed exclusively by women, at Mawkhar in Shillong and Chandmari in Tura.
  6. It was the first state cooperative bank to start mobile banking in rural areas, serving unbanked villages of East Khasi Hills.
  7. It was the first bank in the country to extend housing loans to the general public, before the National Housing Bank came into existence.
  8. It played an active role in introducing IRRI paddy cultivation in flood-prone areas, enabling farmers to harvest before the onset of the monsoon.
  9. It has created direct employment for about 600 people and thousands of job opportunities for educated unemployed youth through self-employment schemes.
  10. It has introduced innovative schemes to increase credit flow and create productive employment, including the Integrated Village Development Scheme, Cooperative Group Financing, the Tourism Development Scheme, and increased allocation under housing and transport loans.
  11. To improve recovery of hardcore overdues, it introduced cash incentives of half to one per cent for office-bearers and others involved in recovery at service cooperative society level.
  12. Under its leadership, cooperative banks in the North Eastern Region, with NABARD, established the Manpower Development and Management Institute in Shillong in 1998, which trains employees, office-bearers and traditional heads across the region.
  13. At its initiative, the North East India Cooperative Development Foundation was established in 1998 as a consortium and forum for cooperative institutions in the region.
  14. It provides guidance and leadership for the short-term cooperative credit structure and the cooperative sector generally in the North Eastern Region, at national forums, refinancing agencies and with the Government of India.
  15. Its performance has been recognised by national cooperative organisations including NAFSCOB, NCARDB and NABARD, through Best Performance Awards.

Mobile banking

To popularise the idea of cooperatives, encourage saving among members and the public in East Khasi Hills District, and bring banking to people’s doorsteps, mobile banking was started under the district’s Integrated Cooperative Development Project on an experimental basis from 5 March 1993, covering nine rural centres held in the premises of service cooperative societies: Mawryngkneng, Sohryngkham, Mawklot, Mawphlang, Mawtawar, Laitkroh, Mawkriah, Sohiong and Tynring.

A mobile van was allotted, with one wing for publicity and one for banking, under the supervision of the Branch Manager, Shillong Main Branch. That van is now out of service and a Tata Sumo is used instead. The intention was that Bank staff would run the service and in time train the cadre secretaries of the societies, so that operations could be handed over and each registered society could run a mini bank at its own office to meet members’ cash and micro-credit needs.

The service is staffed by five Bank employees — a junior assistant, a cashier, a guard, a helper and a driver. Salaries and the running and maintenance of the vehicle are borne entirely by the Bank: about Rs. 0.77 lakh per month in salary and approximately Rs. 10,000 per month in running costs.

At the outset the Bank mobilised deposits of Rs. 1.25 lakh across 372 accounts, mostly savings and recurring deposits. As on 31 December 2009 deposits mobilised had risen to Rs. 167.27 lakh across 3,140 accounts.

Visits are timed to coincide with local market days, so that rural households can withdraw money for their daily requirements and deposit the proceeds of produce sold at the market. Withdrawals above Rs. 50,000 require prior intimation, so that payment can be arranged for the van’s next visit. The exercise serves not only to mobilise deposits but to extend social service to rural people, especially members of cooperative societies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about cooperatives, forming and registering a society, membership, bye-laws and the assistance available from the Department.

Select a question to read the answer. If your question is not covered here, please write to us through the Contact Us page or approach the Assistant Registrar of Cooperative Societies in your district.

Understanding cooperation

Q1What is cooperation?

According to Charles Gide, an economic historian, “Cooperation is self-help and each for all (mutual aid)”. Self-help here means the pride of supplying one's own needs by one's own resources — of being one's own merchant, banker, money lender and employer. “Each for all” means to seek liberation not only for oneself, but for and through others.

Q2What are cooperatives?

According to the International Cooperative Alliance (I.C.A., Manchester, U.K.), 1995, “a cooperative is an autonomous association of persons united voluntarily to meet their common economic, social and cultural needs and aspirations through a jointly owned and democratically controlled enterprise”.

Q3What is a cooperative society?

An autonomous, open and voluntary association of persons of the weaker section of society, formed to meet their common socio-economic and cultural needs and aspirations through a jointly owned and democratically controlled enterprise, in accordance with cooperative values and principles.

Q5What are cooperative values?

Cooperative values are based on self-help, self-responsibility, democracy, equality, equity and solidarity.

Q6What are the principles of cooperation?

Cooperative principles are the set of basic guidelines by which cooperatives put their values into practice. The seven cornerstones are:

  1. Voluntary and open membership
  2. Democratic member control
  3. Member economic participation
  4. Autonomy and independence
  5. Education, training and information
  6. Cooperation among cooperatives
  7. Concern for community
Q8What are the origins of the cooperative movement in India?

Due to the misuse and abuse of the agency system for rural credit by landlords, money lenders and zamindars during British rule in India, there was great consternation and revolt by farmers in some parts of the country. This led to a search for reform models.

Nicholson, a British officer heading a commission, suggested “Find Raiffeisen in India” — meaning the Raiffeisen model of German agricultural credit cooperatives. Alongside other recommendations for agricultural credit, the first Cooperative Society Act of 1904 was enacted to enable the formation of agricultural credit cooperatives. The 1904 Act was later repealed by the Cooperative Societies Act, 1912, which provided for the formation of cooperative societies other than credit societies. In 1919, administrative reforms made cooperatives a provincial subject, making each province responsible for the cooperative movement and its development.

In 1942, the Multi-Unit Cooperative Societies Act was enacted by the Government of India to cover societies whose operations extended to more than one state. In 1984 the Government enacted the comprehensive Multi State Cooperative Societies Act, which repealed the 1942 Act. On the recommendation of the Mirdha Committee and the Model Cooperative Societies Act, the Government enacted the Multi State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002, providing for democratic and autonomous working of cooperatives; it came into force with effect from 19 August 2002.

Q9Which institutions work for the development of the cooperative movement in India?

Some of the important agencies working for the promotion of the cooperative movement in India are the National Cooperative Development Corporation (N.C.D.C.), the National Cooperative Union of India (N.C.U.I.), the National Agricultural Cooperative Marketing Federation of India (N.A.F.E.D.), the Tribal Cooperative Marketing Development Federation of India Limited, Indian Farmers Fertiliser Cooperative Limited (I.F.F.C.O.), Krishak Bharati Cooperative Limited (K.R.I.B.H.C.O.), the National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD), the National Federation of State Cooperative Banks Limited (N.A.F.S.C.O.B.), the Gujarat Cooperative Milk Marketing Federation Limited (A.M.U.L.), the National Dairy Development Board (N.D.D.B.) and the Vaikunth Mehta National Institute of Cooperative Management.

Links to these organisations are available on the Related Websites page.

Forming and registering a society

Q4What is required to form a cooperative society?
  1. Public or community awareness of the socio-economic development potential of the cooperative sector and the cooperative movement.
  2. Awareness of cooperative values and principles.
  3. An initial open and voluntary association of 15 individuals (promoters) to promote the intended cooperative society.

Qualification for membership

  1. An adult — 18 years of age or above.
  2. Of sound mind.
  3. A resident within the intended area of operation.
  4. Not a defaulter of another registered cooperative society (to be certified).
  5. Not insolvent.
  6. Not convicted for misappropriation or moral turpitude.
  7. The fifteen members, known as promoters, will submit their application on plain paper or in the form available at the office of the Assistant Registrar of Cooperative Societies of the respective district, for registration of the proposed society.
Q7What is the procedure for registration of a cooperative society?
  1. On receiving the application, the Assistant Registrar, through his subordinate, will conduct an inquiry to establish the feasibility and economic viability of the proposed society, and whether its proposed bye-laws conform to the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act and Rules. Where the proposed society is a fishery, dairy, poultry, piggery or handloom society, views and comments from the department concerned must be obtained before registration.
  2. Once the inquiry report and the views and comments from the department concerned are found favourable, permission is given to complete the necessary formalities for registration.
  3. A date is fixed for the inaugural general meeting of the promoters, at which members finally decide on the adoption of the bye-laws and on matters concerning day-to-day functioning of the society, including constitution of the managing committee and operation of the bank account.
  4. At the inaugural general meeting, one of the promoters presides. An officer from the Department attends and helps the promoters with organisational matters. At this meeting the members decide what type of society they wish to form, how many persons will join as members, elect the office bearers, and decide the authorised share capital and the share capital to be contributed by each member.
  5. All papers, duly signed by the promoters, are submitted to the Assistant Registrar of Cooperative Societies, who registers the society and its bye-laws after satisfying himself on all points.
  6. The certificate of registration in the form set forth in the schedule is issued by the Assistant Registrar after registration. A copy of the registration with bye-laws is sent to the applicant and to the departments concerned.
Q11What are the steps and checklist for forming a cooperative society?

Important steps involved in forming a cooperative society:

  1. A prescribed application, duly filled in, shall be made to the Registrar of Cooperative Societies.
  2. The application shall be accompanied by four copies of the proposed bye-laws of the society.
  3. Where all the applicants are individuals, the number of applicants shall not be less than ten.
  4. The application shall be signed by every one of the applicants, if the applicants are individuals.
  5. If the applicant is a society, the application shall be signed by a member duly authorised by that society.

For a multi-state cooperative society

  1. An application for registration shall be made in the prescribed form.
  2. The application shall be signed:
    1. where all the members are individuals, by at least fifty persons from each of the states concerned;
    2. where the members are cooperative societies, by duly authorised representatives on behalf of at least five such societies that are not registered in the same state;
    3. where the members are other multi-state cooperative societies and other cooperative societies, by duly authorised representatives of each such society;
    4. where the members are cooperative societies or multi-state cooperative societies and individuals, by at least fifty persons, being individuals, from each of two or more states, and one cooperative society each from two or more states or one multi-state cooperative society.
  3. The application shall be accompanied by four copies of the proposed bye-laws, stating the name of the proposed multi-state cooperative society, its headquarters and registered address, its area of operation, its main objectives, and a certificate from the bank stating the credit balance held in favour of the proposed society.

The relevant application forms can be obtained from the office of the Registrar of Cooperative Societies nearest to you.

Q10Who can become members of a cooperative society in India?

The persons who may become members of a cooperative society at state level are as per the State Act. Generally:

  1. an individual competent to contract, who has attained majority, is of sound mind, and belongs to the class of persons (if any) for whom the society is formed as per its bye-laws;
  2. a society registered or deemed to be registered under the Cooperative Societies Act;
  3. the Government.

No individual shall be eligible for admission as a member of a federal society.

Persons who may become members of a multi-state cooperative society, as per the Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002:

  1. an individual competent to contract under section 11 of the Indian Contract Act;
  2. any multi-state cooperative society or any cooperative society;
  3. the Central Government;
  4. a State Government;
  5. the National Cooperative Development Corporation (N.C.D.C.);
  6. any other corporation owned or controlled by the Government.

No individual person shall be eligible for admission as a member of a national cooperative society or a federal cooperative.

Running a society

Q14Who is responsible for the executive management of a society?

As per the provisions of the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act and the bye-laws of the society, the members of the managing committee elected by the general body meeting are responsible for managing the affairs of the society. Managing committee members are elected annually from among the members of the society at the annual general assembly or general meeting.

Q15What are the financial resources for running a society?
  1. Share capital contributed by members.
  2. Monthly contributions deposited by members.
  3. Borrowing from State Cooperative Banks and other financing agencies.
  4. Financial assistance provided by the State Government and, in some cases, by the Central Government.
Q16How can a registered bye-law be amended? (Act, section 13)

From time to time a society may amend its bye-laws by altering, rescinding or cancelling a clause, or by inserting a new clause as a new bye-law. Rule 9(I) of the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Rules states:

No amendment of the bye-laws of a registered cooperative society shall be valid until such amendment has been registered under the Act [section 13(3)].

Why and what to amend

  1. Modification of existing provisions.
  2. Deletion of existing provisions.
  3. Insertion of new provisions.
  4. Complete amendment, or constituting a new bye-law.

An amendment can be made

  1. at the instance of the society, by a resolution passed by a majority of the members at a general meeting where not less than two-thirds of members are present;
  2. at the direction of the Registrar;
  3. at the suggestion of the affiliating society.
Q17What is the procedure for registering an amendment to the bye-laws?

In every case of amendment of bye-laws, the approval of the general body meeting is needed. The application, in the form set forth in the schedule, for registration of a new or altered bye-law or for the abrogation of an existing bye-law, shall be submitted to the Registrar or the Assistant Registrar of Cooperative Societies. It must be signed by the members of the managing committee and should state:

  1. the date of the meeting of the general assembly at which the amendment was passed;
  2. the number of members on the roll of the society on the date of issue of notice of the general meeting;
  3. the number of members present at the meeting;
  4. the number of members who voted in support of the amendment.

If the Assistant Registrar refuses to register an amendment of bye-laws, he must record in writing the reason for his refusal and communicate his decision to the society.

The application for registration of the amendment should be submitted along with a copy of the resolution passed by the annual general meeting and four copies of the existing bye-laws — a fifth copy is required in the case of an affiliating society. When the Assistant Registrar registers an amendment, he returns the old bye-laws to the society, duly corrected and attested, along with a certificate of registration of the amendment in the form set forth in the schedule.

Q23How are cooperative societies graded?

Audit classification of cooperative societies covers cooperative audit and financial and administrative audit.

Class A

  • Financially strong.
  • Avails no external finance or supervision.
  • Upholds cooperative principles adequately.
  • No overdues.
  • Profit making.

Class B

  • Financially strong.
  • Requires external financial assistance and supervision.
  • Overdues up to 25 per cent of total outstanding.

Class C

  • Financially weak.
  • Requires external financial assistance and supervision.
  • Overdues between 25 and 50 per cent of total outstanding.

Class D

  • Dormant or defunct.
  • Management has collapsed.
  • Overdues above 50 per cent of total outstanding.

Law, assistance and structure in Meghalaya

Q12Which laws regulate cooperative societies in India?
  1. The State Cooperative Societies Acts of individual states — in this State, the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act, 2015.
  2. The Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002, for multi-state cooperative societies with an area of operation in more than one state.

State rules and notifications are published on the Rules and Regulations page.

Q13What is the Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act?

A multi-state cooperative society means a society registered or deemed to be registered under the Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002, and includes a national cooperative society or a federal cooperative.

Q20What are the laws governing cooperatives in the State?

The Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act is supported by the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Rules (the Assam Rules, 1953, as adopted by Meghalaya).

Bye-laws are framed as sub-laws to suit the various types of society within the purview of the Act and Rules. A bye-law cannot override or overrule any provision within the State Cooperative Societies Act and Rules.

Q18What financial assistance does the Government provide to cooperative societies?

To promote and develop cooperative societies in the State, the State Government provides financial assistance in the following forms:

  1. Share capital contribution, to strengthen the share capital base of the society.
  2. Managerial subsidy, to meet the managerial expenses of societies.
  3. Assistance to be used as margin money for borrowing from financial institutions.
  4. Loan and subsidy for construction of godowns or worksheds of primary weavers' societies.
  5. Working capital loan.
  6. Loan for purchase of plant and machinery.
  7. Loan for purchase of furniture and fixtures.
  8. Loan and subsidy for repair and renovation of cooperative godowns.
  9. Subsidy for publicity and motivational programmes.

Full details are on the Financial Assistance and Subsidy Programme pages.

Q19How is the Cooperation Department in Meghalaya organised?

Meghalaya has a separate administrative department for the promotion of the cooperative movement, headed by a Cabinet Minister. In the Secretariat, the Department is headed by the Commissioner and Secretary, supported by officers and staff. At the Directorate level, the Office of the Registrar of Cooperative Societies is headed by the Registrar of Cooperative Societies, assisted by the J.R.C.S., A.R.C.S., S.R.C.S. and other officials and staff.

At the district level there are seven districts headed by Assistant Registrars of Cooperative Societies, and three civil sub-divisions headed by Sub-Registrars of Cooperative Societies. One Zonal Officer in the rank of D.R.C.S. looks after the three districts in Garo Hills. Senior Cooperative Officers and Junior Cooperative Officers look after the welfare of the cooperative societies.

The full structure is set out on the Organisational Setup page.

Q21What kinds of cooperative society can be formed in Meghalaya?

Any type of cooperative society which has as its chief objective the promotion of the economic interest and general welfare of its members in accordance with cooperative principles — or a society established with the object of facilitating the operation of such a society, including a society formed by the division of an existing registered cooperative society or by the amalgamation of two or more existing societies — may be registered under the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act, 2015, with or without limited liability.

Q22How are cooperative societies categorised by status and operation?

Societies are categorised at three levels: state or apex level, district level, and primary or grass-root level.

State / apex level

  1. Meghalaya Cooperative Apex Bank Limited (M.C.A.B.)
  2. Meghalaya State Housing Financing Cooperative Society Limited (M.S.H.F.C.S.)
  3. Meghalaya Apex Handloom Weaving and Handicrafts Cooperative Federation Limited (M.A.H.W.H.C.F.)
  4. Meghalaya State Cooperative Marketing and Consumers' Federation Limited (M.E.C.O.F.E.D.)
  5. Meghalaya State Cadre Management Cooperative Societies (M.S.C.M.C.S.)
  6. Meghalaya State Cooperative Union Limited (M.S.C.U.)

District level — nil.

Primary / grass-root level

A. Credit societies: Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (P.A.C.S.) and Employees' Credit or Thrift Cooperatives (E.C.S.).

B. Non-credit societies: joint and collective farming, processing, marketing, poultry and piggery, fisheries, livestock, consumer, handloom, weaving and handicraft, labour contract, forest labour, housing, transport, women's societies, multipurpose societies and other types.

Q24How does a multi-state cooperative society differ from a company?
Broad differences between a cooperative and a company
# Item Cooperative society Company
1 Object Interest of members and community is paramount — the basic principle of cooperation. Self-interest, either of management or of shareholders; profit as the motive.
2 Management The chairperson is elected by the board of directors from among members. The managing director or chief executive is appointed by the board. Usually the chairperson or managing director is the person holding the maximum number of shares.
3 Number of members Where societies are members of a multi-state cooperative society, two societies from different states must sign the application for registration. Where a multi-state cooperative society is a member, that society and one other society must sign. For individual membership, the minimum is 50 members from each state. In a public limited company the minimum is 7 members; in a private limited company the minimum is 2.
4 Share capital Shares are not issued to the general public by advertisement and can be issued at any time. Shares can be withdrawn by a member or society as prescribed in the bye-laws. Shares are issued to the general public or by invitation. Shares cannot be withdrawn by a shareholder.
5 Share types Only equity shares are available. Equity and preference shares may be issued.
6 Voting rights A member has the right to only one vote, irrespective of the number or denomination of shares held. Voting rights depend directly on shareholding.
7 Control The Central Registrar of Cooperatives advises in the affairs of a multi-state society. Companies are governed by the Company Registrar of the state where the registered office is located.
8 Profit distribution A minimum of 25 per cent of net profits must be transferred to the general reserve, and the maximum dividend cannot exceed 20 per cent. No such restrictions.
9 M.R.T.P. Act Not applicable to multi-state societies. Applicable.
10 Participation of workers Provision exists for workers' participation in management through a representative. No such provision for workers.
11 Taxes Cooperatives are exempt from certain taxes in some states, such as stamp duty. Tax rates also vary. No such exemptions.

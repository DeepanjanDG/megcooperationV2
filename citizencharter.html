Citizen's Charter

Our commitment to the people we serve — what the Department does, how it performs, and how to raise a grievance.

This Department is committed to strengthening the Cooperative Movement through the economic upliftment of rural communities and weaker sections in the State, by organising communities into cooperative ventures that can function as micro-enterprises.

Introduction

The Department of Cooperation is one of the key Departments in the State. It is organised into two divisions — the first tier being the Administrative Department, followed by the Directorate at the cutting edge. It has two attached offices and ten subordinate offices spread across the State for the purpose of coordination, execution and implementation of schemes in their respective jurisdictions. There are eight State Level Cooperative Organisations under the administrative control of the Department, and 2,028 Primary Cooperative Societies in the State as on 15 October 2020.

The Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act, 2015 (Act No. 11 of 2015), which came into effect from 7 October 2015, empowers the Registrar of Cooperative Societies for the purpose of registration, supervision, control, management and conduct of statutory audit for accountability and transparency, besides supporting and counselling deserving cooperative societies for their overall growth and development.

To bring the administration closer to the people, most of the powers of the Registrar of Cooperative Societies have been delegated to the Assistant Registrar of Cooperative Societies at the district level and the Sub-Registrar of Cooperative Societies at the sub-divisional level, supported by field officers in the rank of Senior Cooperative Officers and Junior Cooperative Officers.

Vision

Through cooperatives, to promote sustainable and equitable development and rural prosperity through effective credit support, related services, institution development and other innovative initiatives aimed at making cooperatives the nucleus of economic regeneration in rural areas and engines of growth in urban areas; and to make cooperative societies economically and democratically strong for professionalised services and an empowered rural India.

Mission

To strengthen the Cooperative Movement through the economic upliftment of rural communities and weaker sections in the State, by organising communities into cooperative ventures that can function as micro-enterprises.

Details of business transacted by the Department

The business transacted by the Department and the strategies for business development are embedded in the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act, 2015 and the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Rules (adapted from Assam). The main business transacted is as follows.

  • Formulating cooperative policies for cooperation and cooperative organisations at the State level.
  • Strengthening the Cooperative Movement through State Level Cooperative Societies — the Meghalaya Handloom and Handicraft Cooperative Federation Ltd., the Meghalaya Cooperative Apex Bank Ltd., the Meghalaya State Cooperative Marketing and Consumers' Federation Ltd., the State Housing and Financing Cooperative Society Ltd., the Meghalaya State Cooperative Union, and the Meghalaya Village Development and Promotional Tourism Cooperative Society Ltd.
  • Organisation and registration of new cooperative societies.
  • Statutory audit, conduct of enquiry and inspection, settlement of disputes, dissolution of disputes, recovery of sums due and enforcement of obligations.
  • Registration and amendment of bye-laws.
  • Liquidation.
  • Assisting cooperative societies in the shape of share capital investment for business conducted, and managerial subsidy for capacity building of staff appointed by the society.
  • Development of rural micro-enterprises.
  • Poverty alleviation and employment generation.
  • Rural infrastructure development.
  • Experimenting with new models, products and innovative practices in rural credit and other allied fields.
  • Thrust on rural awareness, education, training and financial services.
  • Enhancing coordination with sectoral nodal departments such as Agriculture, Horticulture, Sericulture, Animal Husbandry and Veterinary, and Industries, for the development of the respective sector cooperatives.
  • Working in close coordination with the Integrated Basin Development and Livelihood Promotion Programme (IBDLP) and Megh-LAMP.

Note. As per the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act, 2015 (sections 80 to 86), the Registrar or any person empowered by him on his behalf shall have full authority to act as a Civil Court on the business transacted by the Department, and no Civil or Revenue Court shall have any jurisdiction over the above-mentioned businesses of the Department.

Some client-centric programmes of the Department

  • Registration of cooperative societies.
  • Financial assistance and managerial subsidy to deserving cooperative societies, under both State schemes and Centrally Sponsored Schemes.
  • Awareness, education and training, promotional activities, and introduction of new technology through tie-ups with other development agencies.
  • Experimenting with new models and innovative practices for rural social and economic upliftment.
  • Operation of the Right to Information.
  • Documentation of cooperative activities and ventures, highlighting cooperative products through brochures and through exhibitions and expos at the national and international level.

Capacity development

The Department provides training to junior officers in the related field for capacity building, and also provides further training such as the higher Diploma Course in Cooperation, deputing officers for short-term training to different institutions for different courses from time to time.

The Department also organises capacity building and training modules for cooperative societies, teaching them new skills and technologies.

Performance

To ensure that each individual and unit performs as per the standard set, the performance of each individual officer is reviewed on a monthly basis at the district and sub-divisional level, and on a bi-monthly basis at the Directorate level.

Monitoring and evaluation

To ensure that the commitment is met and the quality of services is maintained, the Registrar at the Directorate level oversees, monitors and evaluates the performance of every district and sub-division from time to time.

Assistance under various plans is linked to performance indicators such as growth in membership and payment of dividend.

Grievance redressal mechanism and how to access it

  • The Department is prepared and adequately sensitised to redress grievances in a responsible and effective manner, so as to address complaints arising out of the business transacted by the Department.
  • The Department interacts directly at the Directorate level and at subordinate offices for the redressal of complaints.
  • The Department appeals to stakeholders to provide full and complete information on their grievances, to enable timely settlement of the issues involved. It continues to be the Department's endeavour to settle grievances received from stakeholders as early as possible, within a specified time frame.
  • The Citizen's Charter is reviewed annually to further improve our responses to the public.

Integrated Cooperative Development Project

An area-based project approach to developing a district through cooperative effort in agriculture and allied sectors.

The Integrated Cooperative Development Project (ICDP) was introduced to develop a district through cooperative effort in agriculture and allied sectors — fishery, poultry, handloom, rural industries, livestock and consumer activity.

The concept envisages selecting an area at district level, formulating activities and programmes in an integrated manner based on the resources of the area and the needs of the people, and developing those activities through cooperatives.

Adopting this area-based project approach, the National Cooperative Development Corporation, New Delhi, launched a Central Sector scheme for ICDP in West Garo Hills and Jaintia Hills during 1999–2000, in East Garo Hills and West Khasi Hills during 2001–2002, and in South Garo Hills and part of Ri-Bhoi and East Khasi Hills during 2004–2005.

Conditions before implementation

Under the terms of the NCDC’s sanctioning letters, the following had to be put in place before the scheme could be implemented:

  • Constitution of a State Level Coordination Committee under the chairmanship of the Commissioner and Secretary to the Government of Meghalaya, Cooperation Department.
  • Constitution of a District Level Coordination Committee under the chairmanship of the Deputy Commissioner of the district concerned.
  • Appointment of a Project Implementation Agency — the Meghalaya Cooperative Apex Bank Limited, Shillong.
  • Appointment of a Project Implementation Team at district level, headed by a General Manager or Manager, on deputation from the Government or the Bank.
  • Setting up of a Central Monitoring Cell at the Office of the Registrar of Cooperative Societies, Meghalaya, Shillong.

Pattern of funding

  • The NCDC sanctions assistance to the State Government as a loan component and a subsidy component, the subsidy being 28 to 30 per cent of the total block cost and the remainder loan.
  • The State Government in turn provides 50 per cent share capital contribution to the beneficiary cooperative societies and the remaining 50 per cent as loan. No subsidy component is provided to the societies.
  • The subsidy component is provided by the NCDC for the Project Implementation Team, covering manpower development and training for beneficiaries, incentives to apex and primary societies, and the management cost of the team.
  • The State Government accords sanction and releases funds according to the year-wise pattern of funding by the NCDC, and is reimbursed by the NCDC.

Impact of the scheme

Of the projects listed above, three were under implementation: East Garo Hills, South Garo Hills, and part of Ri-Bhoi and East Khasi Hills.

East Garo Hills District

The project was implemented through the Project Implementation Team appointed by the Meghalaya Cooperative Apex Bank Limited, Shillong, across different sectors according to the year-wise pattern of funding.

  1. 14 Primary Agricultural Credit Societies and Service Cooperative Societies were provided with office-cum-godown, furniture and fixtures, cash counters and safes.
  2. 14 such societies were provided with power tillers.
  3. 17 such societies were provided with margin money for raising working capital loans.

These activities greatly helped the members of the societies, in ways that did not exist before the project. Under the allied sector, six to eight societies took up relevant activities, for which the project provided construction of worksheds for handloom cooperatives, construction and repair of ponds, and necessary implements and equipment. The project also provided margin money as share capital contribution, which societies could link with the loan component to increase working capital according to their viability.

South Garo Hills District

  1. 5 Primary Agricultural Credit Societies and Service Cooperative Societies were provided with office-cum-godown.
  2. 5 such societies were provided with furniture and fixtures to enable them to start an office.
  3. 5 such societies were provided with margin money.

Under allied activities, handloom, dairy farming, spice processing, fishery and distribution of consumer articles were taken up by the relevant societies. Margin money in the shape of share capital contribution was provided so that societies could link it with the loan component. Implementation across sectors reached about 55 per cent within the third year, and the cooperative sector across the district took on a new shape once societies were identified under the scheme.

Ri-Bhoi and part of East Khasi Hills

  1. 2 Primary Agricultural Credit Societies and Service Cooperative Societies were identified and sanctioned for construction of office-cum-godown and repair of godown.
  2. 4 societies were identified and sanctioned for furniture and fixtures.
  3. 2 societies were identified and sanctioned for purchase of pump sets and sprayers.
  4. 4 societies were identified and sanctioned for cash counters and safes.

In addition, 5 societies were identified and sanctioned for share capital contribution as margin money, to be linked with the loan component.

In the other allied sectors, societies were identified under the funding pattern, with sanction from the Project Implementation Agency (Meghalaya Cooperative Apex Bank Ltd) as follows:

  • Fishery — 4 societies for pond development, office-cum-shed, appliances, nets and margin money.
  • Handloom — 3 societies for workshed, 30 looms and margin money.
  • Rural industry — 1 society for a cane and bamboo shed and margin money.
  • Women’s cooperatives — 1 society for equipment and margin money for a tailoring unit.
  • Processing — 2 societies for a processing unit.
  • Poultry — 2 societies for shed and margin money.
  • Piggery — 5 societies for shed and margin money.
  • Dairy — 2 societies for a collection centre, milk van and margin money.

ICDP East Garo Hills closed on 31 March 2009, after a two-year extension of its validity period. For ICDP South Garo Hills and ICDP Ri-Bhoi and part of East Khasi Hills, the NCDC accorded sanction in 2004–2005, but actual implementation began only in 2005–2006 and one year had already lapsed. Both were to be completed by 31 March 2009, with a further year under the NCDC’s funding pattern yet to be released by the State Government to the Project Implementation Agency, and an extension of the validity period to be obtained by that Agency.

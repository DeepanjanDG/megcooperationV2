Introduction

The cooperative movement in Meghalaya — its principles, its history, and the societies that make it up today.

The Department works for the development of cooperatives in the state. The cooperative movement is rooted in people’s organisation, built on seven basic principles.

  • 1Voluntary and open membership
  • 2Democratic member control
  • 3Members’ economic participation
  • 4Autonomy and independence
  • 5Education, training and information
  • 6Cooperation among cooperatives
  • 7Concern for the community

Cooperatives are autonomous associations of persons united voluntarily to meet their common economic, social and cultural needs and aspirations through a jointly owned and democratically controlled enterprise. They imbibe the values of self-help, self-responsibility, democracy, equality, equity and solidarity.

How the movement developed

Due to the misuse and abuse of the agency system for rural credit by landlords and money lenders during British rule in India, there was great consternation and revolt by farmers in some parts of the country. This led to a search for reform models, and to the first cooperative legislation.

Milestones in cooperative law and in Meghalaya
1904The first Cooperative Societies Act is enacted, enabling the formation of agricultural credit cooperatives in response to the misuse of the agency system for rural credit.
1912The 1904 Act is repealed by the Cooperative Societies Act, 1912, which allows the formation of cooperative societies other than credit societies.
1919Administrative reforms make cooperation a provincial subject, giving each province responsibility for the cooperative movement.
1942 – 1984The Multi-Unit Cooperative Societies Act, 1942 covers societies operating in more than one state; it is repealed by the comprehensive Multi State Cooperative Societies Act, 1984.
1948The office of the Assistant Registrar of Cooperative Societies is established in Shillong.
1950After independence Assam passes its own Assam Cooperative Societies Act, 1950.
1970On becoming an autonomous state on 2 April 1970, Meghalaya inherits 472 credit cooperative societies.
1971 – 72Meghalaya has 953 cooperative societies with 57,000 members, Rs. 32 lakh of share capital and Rs. 133 lakh of working capital.
1974A master plan is formulated by the Reserve Bank of India with the Registrar and the Meghalaya Cooperative Apex Bank, leading to 180 re-organised Service Cooperative Societies (PACS).
1976Meghalaya adopts the legacy Assam Act.
2002The Multi State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002 comes into force on 19 August 2002, providing for democratic and autonomous working of cooperatives.
2015The Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act, 2015 governs registration, audit and regulation of societies in the state.

Historically the societies were not in good health, owing to poor management, lack of supervision, the absence of full-time trained secretaries, the absence of a link between credit and marketing, low yields from traditional cultivation, limited marketing facilities for farm produce, and a lack of cooperative education.

The 1974 master plan resulted in the emergence of 180 re-organised Service Cooperative Societies — popularly known as PACS — across the whole state. All of them had full-time paid secretaries and were designed for multipurpose activity: dispensation of credit, distribution of fertiliser, improved seeds and other inputs, supply of consumer goods, and marketing of surplus agricultural produce.

Besides these, Meghalaya has many other types of cooperative society — housing, handloom, industrial, fisheries, consumer, labour contract, transport, poultry, women’s, joint farming, processing, dairy and piggery societies. These function according to the aims and objectives in their bye-laws and in accordance with the State Cooperative Act and Rules.

How the Department is arranged

The Department of Cooperation operates as the administrative department in the Secretariat, and the Office of the Registrar of Cooperative Societies is the head of department that administers the cooperative societies. See the organisational setup for the full structure.

Apex level cooperative societies

The state has the following apex level societies:

Meghalaya Co-operative Apex Bank Limited

Provides short-term, medium-term and long-term credit to farmers who are members of cooperative societies across the state.

Learn more  →

Meghalaya Co-operative Federation and Marketing Societies Limited (MECOFED)

Markets the agricultural produce of affiliated societies, distributes fertilisers and inputs to farmers, and supplies consumer goods and essential commodities in urban and rural areas.

Learn more  →

Meghalaya State Housing and Financial Co-operative Society Limited

Organised with the primary housing cooperative societies at base level to provide housing loans and house construction facilities to the weaker sections in urban and rural areas.

Learn more  →

Meghalaya Apex Handloom Weavers Co-operative and Handicraft Federation Limited

Supplies quality yarn to primary handloom societies and markets the finished products. Handloom occupies an important role in small-scale and village cottage industry in the state.

Learn more  →

Meghalaya State Co-operative Union

Established in 1978 to conduct training courses that equip members to participate in the affairs of their societies, including short courses at district and sub-divisional level.

Learn more  →

Meghalaya Supervision and Cadre Management Society

Formerly implemented assistance for society secretaries, training and education programmes. Following national recommendations, control and direction of secretaries now rests with the societies that employ them.

Learn more  →
